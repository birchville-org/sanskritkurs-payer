# Lesson 54

## 54.1. Preliminary Remarks

With Lesson 54, the second semester of university Sanskrit instruction begins. From this point onward, the course runs concurrently with the main topic of the semester: the reading of the entire *Bhagavadgītā*. The learning objective is to achieve such fluency in reading a medium-difficulty text that, during the final third of the semester, large portions of the *Bhagavadgītā* can be translated extemporaneously. At the beginning of the course, vocabulary lists were distributed; later, students were required to consult appropriate dictionaries (Monier-Williams, Apte, PW) independently.

The course on the *Bhagavadgītā* will not be made available online for the time being.

The lessons of the Sanskrit course cover topics of Sanskrit grammar that have not yet been addressed.

**On desiderative stems (इच्छाप्रकृति) ("to wish to do something"; "to be about to do something") the suffix -u is added for the formation of nominal agents.**
:::

## 54.3. Meter II: The Epic Triṣṭubh and Jagatī

The Triṣṭubh ("Three-jauchzer") appears in the epics amidst the usual śloka sections precisely at points where the mood or action experiences a particular surge or conclusion.

The Triṣṭubh stanza consists of four eleven-syllable pādas, which do not differ from one another in structure.

The Triṣṭubh has two basic schemas, depending on whether the caesura (word end, compositional junction, or before certain suffixes such as -tara, -tama, etc.) occurs after the 4th or 5th syllable of the pāda.

Schema I:

::: indent
̽ ˉ ̽ ˉ / ̽ ˘ˉ***ˉ˘ˉˉ***
:::

Schema II:

::: indent
̽ ˉ ̽ ˉ ̽  / ̽ ˘***ˉ˘ˉˉ***
:::

The quantity of the last four syllables is thus identical in both schemas.

Jagatī basic form: like Triṣṭubh, but 12-syllabic. The last 5 syllables of each pada exhibit the following quantities:

::: indent
***ˉ˘ˉ˘ˉ***
:::

Additionally, there exists the so-called typically superfluous Triṣṭubh with a five-syllabic initial segment, which continues like a Triṣṭubh with a four-syllabic initial segment:

::: indent
ˉ ̽ ˉ ̽  / ̽ ˘ˉ***ˉ˘ˉˉ***
:::

In later periods, the forms of the Triṣṭubh are fixed according to a strict schema of long and short syllables; the caesura no longer plays a role. The most important later forms are:

a) Indravajrā

::: indent
***ˉ***ˉ˘ˉ,ˉ˘˘ˉ˘ˉˉ
:::

b) Upendravajrā

::: indent
***˘***ˉ˘ˉ,ˉ˘˘ˉ˘ˉˉ
:::

c) Upajātī

::: indent
Pādas in Indravajrā and pādas in Upendravajrā are mixed into a single stanza.
:::

::: grammar-box
**Mnemonic verses:**

**स्यादिन्द्रवज्रा यदि तौ जगौ गः ।**
:::

::: indent
"It is an Indravājrā if the syllables in all pādas are ta ta ja ga ga."
:::

उपेन्द्रवज्रा प्रथमे लघौ सा ।

::: indent
"An Upendravajrā is like an Indravajrā that has a light syllable at the beginning."
:::

अनन्तरोदीरितलक्ष्मभाजौ  
पदौ यदीयावुपजातयस्ताः ।  
इत्थं किलान्यास्वपिमिश्रितासु  
वदन्ति जातिष्विदमिदमेवनाम ॥

::: indent
"If two pādas, which possess the characteristics of Indravajrā and Upendravajrā respectively, are involved in a verse, then they are Upajātis. Even if other meters are mixed in a verse, it is called Upajāti."
:::

## 54.5. Types of Aorist Formation (लुङ्)

::: grammar-box
**All types of aorist formation (लुङ्) share the augment *a-*, which is prefixed according to the same rules as in the imperfect (लङ्).**
:::

In Sanskrit, only the indicative and optative moods are commonly used for the aorist.

::: grammar-box
**The following types of aorist formation (लुङ्) exist:**
:::

1.  **Non-sibilant (asigmatic) aorists:**
    *   54.1. Root aorist: Augment + Root + Secondary ending
    *   54.2. Thematic-vowel aorist: Augment + Root + ***a*** + Secondary ending
    *   54.3. Reduplicated aorist: Augment + ***reduplicated*** root + ***a*** + Secondary ending
2.  **Sibilant (sigmatic) aorists:** Here, *s* or a syllable containing *s* (ṣ) precedes the personal ending.
    *   54.4. *-s*-aorist: Augment + Root + ***s*** + Secondary ending
    *   54.5. *-iṣ*-aorist: Augment + Root + ***i*** + ***ṣ*** + Secondary ending
    *   54.6. *-siṣ*-aorist: Augment + Root + ***siṣ*** + Secondary ending
    *   54.7. *-sa*-aorist: Augment + Root + ***s*** + ***a*** + Secondary ending

For the distribution of roots among the various formation types, see the respective sections for each type.

## 54.6. Root Aorist

::: grammar-box
**Formation:**

**Augment + Root + Secondary Ending**
:::

The ending for the 3rd person plural is *-ur*. The Ātmanepada (middle voice) conjugation is not commonly used.

Example:

पा 1P "to drink"

::: grammar-box
|   | Singular (एकवचनम्) | Plural (बहुवचनम्) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1\. Third Person (तृतीयः) | अपाम   | |
| a-pā + am | अपाम | | |
| 2\. Second Person (मध्यमः) | अपास् | अपात |
| 3\. First Person (प्रथमः) | अपात् | अपुर्   |
| a-p-ur (Guna/Reduction stage!) | | | |
:::

The Root Aorist is formed from only 12 roots:

1.  गा 3 "to go"
2.  घ्रा 1P "to smell"
3.  छो 6 "to cut" (अच्छात्)
4.  दा 3U "to give"
5.  दो 1, 4 "to cut"
6.  धा 3U "to place"
7.  धे 1 "to suck" (अधात्)
8.  पा 1P "to drink"
9.  शो 4P "to sharpen"
10. सो "to finish"
11. स्था 1P "to stand"
12. भू 1P "to be"

For the root भू 1P, the Root Aorist is formed as follows:

::: grammar-box
|   | Singular (एकवचनम्) | Plural (बहुवचनम्) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1\. Third Person (तृतीयः) | अभूवम् | अभूम |
| 2\. Second Person (मध्यमः) | अभू्स् | अभूत |
| 3\. First Person (प्रथमः) | अभूत् | ***अभूवन्*** (!!!) |
:::

### 54.6.1. The Aorist of the 3rd Person Singular Passive

A special form of the root aorist is the aorist of the 3rd person singular passive. This can be formed from all roots.

::: grammar-box
**Formation:**

**Augment + Root + i**

**The root takes the following shape:**
:::

Guṇa grade:

*   Roots with a short vowel (except *a*) before a simple consonant, as well as जन् and most roots ending in -am  
    Examples:  
    भिद् 7U: अभेदि "he was split"  
    तुद् 6U: अतोदि "he was struck"
    

Vrddhi grade:

*   Roots with a final vowel  
    Examples:  
    नी 1U: अनायि "he was led"  
    स्तु 2U: अस्तावि "she was praised"  
    कृ 8U: अकारि "it was made"  
     
    
*   Roots with *a* before a simple consonant (except जन् and roots ending in -am)  
    Example:  
    वद् 1P: अवादि "it was said"  
    but:  
    जन् 4Ā: अजनि "she was born"  
     
    
*   some roots ending in -am  
    Example:  
    गम् अगामि
    

Insertion of *y* before the ending:

*   Roots ending in -ā, -e, -ai, -o insert a *y* between the root's -ā and the ending:  
    Example:  
    दा 3U: अदायि "it was given"
    

Nasal infix:

*   some roots have a nasal infix:  
    Example:  
    लभ् 1Ā: अलम्भि alongside अलाभि : "it was received"
    

(The other forms of the passive in the aorist are expressed through Ātmanepada forms).
