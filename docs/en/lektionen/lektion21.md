# Lesson 21

## 21.1. Further Present Stems in -nt

### 21.1.1. Present Participle (लडादेशः) Parasmaipada

:::: grammar-box
This participle is a nominal formation derived from the present stem, thus constituting a true participle. It functions as an adjective indicating that someone or something is currently performing the action expressed by the verbal root (+ preverb) while another event is taking place. It can also express a continuous state.

**Examples:**

::: indent
"While the father reads, he smokes = the reading father smokes"  
"a being (= real, true) friend"
:::
::::

## 21.2. Sandhi for Final Nasals

:::: grammar-box
**After a short vowel, final nasals (except -m) are doubled before an initial vowel.**

**Example:**

::: indent
**जयन् + अरिः** » **जयन्नरिः** "the victorious foe"
:::
::::

## 21.3. Polite Forms of Address

:::: grammar-box
**In Sanskrit, one can address someone in the second person singular without being impolite. However, if one wishes to be polite, a noun meaning "Venerable" or similar can be used, and the verb can be put into the third person singular or plural, or a passive construction can be employed. The escalation of politeness through the use of person in address is roughly as follows:**

::: indent
**2nd sg. » 2nd pl. » 3rd sg. with corresponding noun » 3rd pl. with corresponding noun**
:::

**The most important such polite noun is भवन्त् (bhavant), fem.: भवती (bhatī). Its usage corresponds to our polite "Sie" (formal "you").**
::::

This **भवन्त्** (bhavant) is a contraction of **भगवन्त्** (bhagavant); its declension follows that of nouns ending in **-vant** (see [Lesson 13](lektion13.md)). This **भवन्त्** (bhavant) must be distinguished from the present participle of **भू** (bhū, "to be") **भवन्त्** (bhavant): the nominative singular masculine of **भवन्त्** (bhavant, "Sir") is **भवान्** (bhavān), whereas that of the participle **भवन्त्** (bhavant, "being") is **भवन्** (bhavan).

**Examples:**

::: indent
**किं भवान् करोति** (kiṃ bhavān karoti) = **किं भवता क्रियते** (kiṃ bhavatā kriyate) = "What are you doing?"
:::

More politely:

::: indent
**किं भवन्तः कुर्वन्ति** (kiṃ bhavantaḥ kurvanti) = **किं भवद्भिः क्रियते** (kiṃ bhavadbhiḥ kriyate)
:::

Feminine:

::: indent
**किं भवती करोति** (kiṃ bhatī karoti) = **किं भवत्या क्रियते** (kiṃ bhatyā kriyate)  
**किं भवत्यः कुर्वन्ति** (kiṃ bhatyaḥ kurvanti) = **किं भवतीभिः क्रियते** (kiṃ bhatībhiḥ kriyate)
:::

Other words that can be used similarly to **भवन्त्** (bhavant):

*   **आर्य** (ārya, fem.: **आर्या** (āryā)) "Noble." E.g., **यदार्य इच्छति** (yad ārya icchati) "What you wish."
*   **महाभाग** (mahābhāga) "One whose share/fortune is great = Distinguished." Often used by women when addressing or speaking about men of good standing. Very common in modern spoken Sanskrit.

If one wishes to express not only politeness but also reverence for someone, **अत्रभवन्त्** (atra bhavant) is used for someone who is present or nearby, and **तत्रभवन्त्** (tatra bhavant) for someone absent or distant. **अत्रभवन्त्** (atra bhavant) and **तत्रभवन्त्** (tatra bhavant) can be translated as "You," "Your Venerableness," "Your Excellency," etc.:

::: indent
**किमत्रभवत्यत्रभवतां भार्या** (kim atra bhaty atra bhavatāṃ bhāryā) = "Is the gracious (here present) lady your wife?"  
**किं तत्रभवतां कुशलवृत्तम्** (kiṃ tatra bhavatāṃ kuśalavṛttam) (in a letter or telephone conversation) = "How are you?"
:::
