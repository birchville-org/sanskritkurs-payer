## 38.2. Sound Change of Nasals within Words

::: grammar-box
Nasals within words undergo assimilation to the following consonant, meaning they are replaced by the nasal corresponding to that consonant.

After *c-* and *j-*, *-n-* is replaced by *-ñ-*.
:::

## 38.3. Declension of Stems Ending in -n

### 38.3.1. Nouns ending in -an, as well as nouns ending in -man or -van after a vowel

::: grammar-box
Nouns ending in **-an**, as well as nouns ending in **-man** or **-van** after a vowel, have three stems:


  
    
      Strong stem
      -ān
      -mān
      -vān
      
        Nom., Acc., Voc. sg. m. f. 
        Nom., Acc., Voc. dual m. f. 
        Nom., Voc. pl. m. f. 
        Nom., Acc. pl. n.
      
    
    
      Medium weak stem
      
        -a 
        (from -*n)
      
      
        -ma 
        (from -*mn)
      
      
        -va 
        (from -*vn)
      
      
        Remaining cases before consonant-initial endings 
        Optionally also Loc. sg. m. n. f.
      
    
    
      Weakest stem
      -n
      -mn
      -vn
      
        Remaining cases before vowel-initial endings
      
    
  


The endings are regular. The nominative singular m. f. is formed without the final **-n**.
:::

Examples:

राजन् m. "king"

*   strong stem: राजान्
*   medium stem: राज
*   weakest stem: राज्ञ्

*   strong stem: सीमान्
*   medium stem: सीम
*   weakest stem: सीम्न्

*   strong stem: नामान्
*   medium stem: नाम
*   weakest stem: नाम्न्

### 38.3.2. Nouns ending in -man or -van after a consonant

::: grammar-box
*   Strong stem: -mān, -vān
*   Weak stem:
    *   before endings beginning with a consonant: -ma, -va (from -\*mn, -\*vn)
    *   before endings beginning with a vowel: ***\-man, -van***

Otherwise, formation follows the rules under 3.1.
:::

Examples:

आत्मन् (ā́tman) n. "soul"

*   strong stem: आत्मान् (ātmān)
*   weak stem:
    *   before consonant: आत्म (ātma)
    *   before vowel: आत्मन् (ātman)

ब्रह्मन् (brahmn) n.: The Absolute, the Veda, Brahman

*   strong stem: ब्रह्मान् (brahmān)

    *   before consonant: ब्रह्म (brahma)

### 38.3.3. Masculine and Neuter Nouns in -in, -min, -vin

::: grammar-box
These nouns exhibit ***no*** stem gradation (apophony).

The nominative singular masculine and nominative/accusative plural neuter forms are modeled on the -a-stems (through lengthening of the -i-), as is the stem in ***\-i-*** before consonantal endings.

The feminine is formed with the suffix -ī: e.g., बलिनी
:::

Example:

बलिन् m.n. "strong, powerful (characterized by special बल, possessing बल)"

## 38.4. On Nominal Derivation: तद्धित Suffix -in

::: grammar-box
The (highly important!) तद्धित suffix **-in** is used to derive adjectives from nouns, with the meaning:

**characterized by, possessing**

Originally, the formation with the suffix **-इन्** differed from that with **-मन्त्/-वन्त्** in that **-इन्** denoted characterization by something special, whereas **-मन्त्/-वन्त्** expressed possession of, or characterization by, something ordinary or general.
:::

Example:

हस्तिन् m.: the one characterized by a special hand = the elephant (for its hand is no ordinary hand, but a trunk)

Fig.: हस्ती

::: grammar-box
Adjectives ending in **-इन्** are frequently used to form compounds.
:::

Example:

सत्यवादिन् from सत्यवाद m. "speaking the truth": "one characterized by speaking the truth = one who always speaks the truth"

## 38.5. Syntactic Notes on नामन्

::: grammar-box
To express "someone named N. N.", the following construction is used:

**N.N. (in the nominative) नाम**

Literally: "the name is/was N.N." This is thus an interpolated nominal sentence.
:::

Example:

::: indent
आसीद्राजा नलो नाम वीरसेनसुतो बली । "There was once a king ***named Nala***, the strong son of Vīrasena."
:::

Naturally, the same meaning can be conveyed using a bahuvrīhi compound:

::: indent
देवदत्तनामा पुरुषः "a man whose name is Devadatta"

मदयन्तिकानामनी बाला "a girl whose name is Madayantika"
:::

## 38.6. Syntactic Notes on आत्मन्

::: grammar-box
**आत्मन्** ***masculine*** can be used in the ***singular*** as a reflexive pronoun for all three genders, numbers (including dual and plural), and persons.
:::

Examples:

::: indent
आत्मन्येषा दोषं न पश्यति । "She sees no fault in herself."

आत्मानं स्तुवन्ति । "They praise themselves."
:::

::: grammar-box
The genitive (षष्ठी) **आत्मनस्** can therefore stand for "my/your/his/... own."
:::

Example:

::: indent
आत्मनो गृहं प्रविशति । "He enters his own house."
:::

## 38.7. Consonant-final stems as the first member of compounds

::: grammar-box
Stems ending in a consonant appear as the first member of a compound in the (weak) stem that they assume before the ending *-su* of the Locative (सप्तमी) Plural.
:::

Example:

::: indent
***राज***पुत्र "king's son"
:::

## 38.8. Stems in -an as the Second Member of a Bahuvrīhi

::: grammar-box
As the second member of a Bahuvrīhi, a stem in -an can be used for all three genders. However, the feminine is usually formed from the weakest masculine stem by means of the suffix -ī.
:::

Example:

::: indent
दुर्णाम्नी "one whose name is bad; a disease-demoness"
:::

## 38.12. Translation Exercise

***daśa*** fools

The actions of fools are generally amusing. For example -- once ***ten*** fools set out on a journey to a distant land. After having traveled some distance, a shallow river appeared before them. ***With their arms***, they somehow crossed the river and reached the other side.

Among them was an old man. He wondered ***whether*** ***all*** had reached the bank and ***counting*** them one by one. However, he counted only ***nine***. Then he cried out: "Alas, ***we*** set out as ***ten***. Now we are only ***nine***. Surely one of us has drowned in the river. ***Search for him!***"

Then ***each one*** of them also performed a count. However, only ***nine*** were visible. Then a great uproar arose among them, ***as they were in a state of confusion***. Not far from there, the hermitage of a certain sage existed. The sage, who lived there, seeing their agitation, laughed loudly. Hearing the sound of the sage's laughter, the ***fools*** ***energetically*** approached him and asked the reason for his laughter. The sage said: "Alas, ***you*** are ignorant of the self. ***None*** of ***you*** counted himself. Therefore, this confusion has arisen." Hearing this, the fools, ashamed and with downcast eyes, departed. (Sanskrit First Model)
