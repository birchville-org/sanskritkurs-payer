# Lesson 5

## 5.1. Nominal compounds (samāsa m. = ⟪समास⟫)

The formation of word compounds on a very large scale is a characteristic feature of Sanskrit.

The most important forms of compound formation are:
**copulative compounds** (dvandva n. = ⟪द्वन्द्व⟫)
**determinative compounds** (tatpuruṣa m. = ⟪तत्पुरुष⟫)
**Possessive compounds** (bahuvrīhi m. = ⟪बहुव्रीहि⟫)

(See later for the last two mentioned!)


## 5.2. On Sanskrit terminology for compounds

**ghaṭakapadāni** n. pl. = ⟪घटकपदानि⟫ : Members of a compound
**vigrahavākyam** n. = ⟪विग्रहवाक्यम्⟫ : Analysis of a compound
**nityasamāsaḥ** m. = ⟪नित्यसमासः⟫ : Compound for which there is no vigrahavākya or whose vigrahavākya cannot be formed with the words of the compound.
**aluksamāsaḥ** m. = ⟪अलुक्समासः⟫ : Compound in which the first member retains a case ending
**luksamāsaḥ** m. = ⟪लुक्समासः⟫ : Compound whose first members are without case endings (the normal case)
**madhyamapadalopī** m. = ⟪मध्यमपदलोपी⟫ : Compound in which one or more middle members are omitted


## 5.3. Copulative compounds (Dvandva n. = ⟪द्वन्द्व⟫)

Copulative compounds serve to link grammatically similar, coordinated members (nouns or adjectives).

::: grammar-box
**A Dvandva denotes:**

1. **either the cumulative compound of persons, things, or qualities designated by its individual members:**
Itaretaradvandva = ⟪इतरेतरद्वन्द्व⟫

2. **or it combines these persons, things, or qualities into a single unit:**
Samāhāradvandva ("collective dvandva") = ⟪समाहारद्वन्द्व⟫

**In the first case (Itaretaradvandva):**
The dvandva takes the grammatical gender of its last member and the endings of the dual (for two items) or plural (for more than two items). Singular endings are also permissible.

**In the second case (Samāhāradvandva):**
The dvandva is generally neuter (regardless of the gender of the last member) and stands in the singular.

**The resolution:**
Occurs with "and" (**ca** = ⟪च⟫), occasionally with "or" (**vā** = ⟪वा⟫) or "each".
:::
See later for dual dvandvas!

### 5.3.1. Padas of Compounds

The padas of compounds (not only dvandvas) generally take the form of the unchanged **nominal stem**. The individual members of compounds are joined according to the rules of **sentence sandhi** (i.e., the sandhi we have learned so far, as opposed to word sandhi, which applies within a word before formative elements).

### 5.3.2. Plural Itaretaradvandva

Examples of plural dvandvas:

**brāhmaṇa-kṣatriya-vaiśya-śūdrāḥ** = ⟪ब्राह्मणक्षत्रियवैश्यशूद्राः⟫
> = brāhmaṇāḥ kṣatriyā vaiśyāḥ śūdrāś ca = ⟪ब्राह्मणाः क्षत्रिया वैश्याः शूद्राश्च⟫
> (Brahmins, Kṣatriyas, Vaiśyas and Śūdras)
> 
> or = brāhmaṇaḥ kṣatriyo vaiśyaḥ śūdraś ca = ⟪ब्राह्मणः क्षत्रियो वैश्यः शूद्रश्च⟫
> ((a/the) Brahmin, Kṣatriya, Vaiśya and Śūdra)

**kavi-guravaḥ** = ⟪कविगुरवः⟫
> = kavayo guravaś ca = ⟪कवयो गुरवश्च⟫
> (poets and teachers [Plural])
> (Note: "A poet and a teacher" would have to be in the Dual!)

**For the order of members in a Dvandva compound, among other rules apply:**
The designation of the different castes (**varṇa**) should be ordered according to their hierarchy (highest first).
The name of the older brother should precede the name of the younger brother.
In general, the more important word should be placed at the beginning.
Words ending in **-i** or **-u** should precede.
Words ending in **-a** and beginning with a vowel should precede.
Words containing fewer syllables should precede.

Where two of the three last-mentioned prescriptions can apply simultaneously, always follow the rule given later. (Examples of Dualdvandva for these rules in Kielhorn, Grammar § 570).


## 5.4. Vocabulary

**Learn the following words:**

**abhiniveṣa** m. = ⟪अभिनिवेष⟫ : inclination, tenacity, insistence; specifically: attachment to the body as if it were something one's own.

**kāma** m. = ⟪काम⟫ : wish, desire, desired gift, sensual pleasure, love; the god of love Kāma.

**krodha** m. = ⟪क्रोध⟫ : anger.

**kleśa** m. = ⟪क्लेश⟫ : torment, affliction.

**trayī** f. = ⟪त्रयी⟫ : triad; in particular the three Vedas (Ṛgveda, Sāmaveda, Yajurveda).

**daṇḍanīti** f. = ⟪दण्डनीति⟫ : Politics (a Tatpuruṣa compound from daṇḍa m. = "stick, power, rule, punishment" and nīti f. = "right guidance").

**dvijāti** 3 / **dvija** 3 = ⟪द्विजाति⟫ / ⟪द्विज⟫ : Twice-born.

**dveṣa** m. = ⟪द्वेष⟫ : Hatred.

**maitrī** f. = ⟪मैत्री⟫ : Friendship, friendliness, friendly goodwill.

**rāga** m. = ⟪राग⟫ : (red) color, passion, love.

**lobha** m. = ⟪लोभ⟫ : Greed, avarice.

**varṇa** m. = ⟪वर्ण⟫ : Color, genus, social class.

**vārttā** f. = ⟪वार्त्ता⟫ : Livelihood, economics (economy).

**vidyā** f. = ⟪विद्या⟫ : Knowledge, science.

**avidyā** f. = ⟪अविद्या⟫ : Ignorance, non-knowledge.

**ca** = ⟪च⟫ : and.
(It is placed after the word it connects. When connecting multiple words, ideally stands behind the first word of the last linking element: brāhmaṇāḥ kṣatriyā vaiśyāḥ śūdrāś ca = ⟪ब्राह्मणाः क्षत्रिया वैश्याः शूद्राश्च⟫).

**ca ... ca** = ⟪च⟫ ... ⟪च⟫ : both ... and.

(Explanation: trayas = ⟪त्रयस्⟫ = "three")

(Kauṭilīya-arthaśāstra 1.3.1. / in good Sanskrit: sāmārgyajurvedās trayī)

(in good Sanskrit: ⟪सामार्यजुर्वेदास्त्रयी⟫)

   kāmakrodhalobhāḥ
⟪कामक्रोधलोभाः⟫ ||

(Explanation: brahmavihāra: "abiding states of Brahma", also called the "Immeasurables". They are Buddhist meditation practices.)

(Yogasūtra 2,3)

(Explanation: pañca = "five")

7. ānvīkṣikītrayīvārttādaṇḍanitayo vidyāḥ.
(According to Kauṭilīya-arthaśāstra 1.2.1.)
⟪आन्वीक्षिकीत्रयीवार्त्तादण्डनितयो विद्याः⟫ ||
