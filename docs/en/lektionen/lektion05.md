## 5.1. Nominalkomposita (samāsa m. = समास)

The extensive formation of word compounds is a characteristic feature of Sanskrit.

The most important forms of compound formation are:
- **copulative compounds** (dvandva n. = द्वन्द्व)
- **determinative compounds** (tatpuruṣa m. = तत्पुरुष)
- **possessive compounds** (bahuvrīhi m. = बहुव्रीहि)

(See later for the latter two!)

---

## 5.2. Zur Sanskrit-Terminologie für Komposita

- **ghaṭakapadāni** n. pl. = घटकपदानि : Members of a compound
- **vigrahavākyam** n. = विग्रहवाक्यम् : Analysis (or explication) of a compound
- **nityasamāsaḥ** m. = नित्यसमासः : A compound for which no *vigrahavākya* exists, or whose *vigrahavākya* cannot be constructed using the words of the compound.
- **aluksamāsaḥ** m. = अलुक्समासः : A compound in which the first member retains a case ending
- **luksamāsaḥ** m. = लुक्समासः : A compound whose first members lack case endings (the normal case)
- **madhyamapadalopī** m. = मध्यमपदलोपी : A compound in which one or more middle members are elided

## 5.3. Kopulative Komposita (Dvandva n. = द्वन्द्व)

Copulative compounds serve to link grammatically homogeneous, coordinated constituents (nouns or adjectives).

::: grammar-box
**A Dvandva denotes:**

1. **either the summative connection of the persons, things, or qualities designated by its individual constituents:**
   Itaretaradvandva = इतरेतरद्वन्द्व

2. **or it combines these persons, things, or qualities into a single unit:**
   Samāhāradvandva ("Summative Dvandva") = समाहारद्वन्द्व

**In the first case (Itaretaradvandva):**
The Dvandva assumes the grammatical gender of its final constituent and takes the endings of the dual (in the case of two items) or the plural (in the case of more than two items). Singular endings are also permissible.

**In the second case (Samāhāradvandva):**
The Dvandva is generally neuter (regardless of the gender of the final constituent) and appears in the singular.

**The resolution:**
Is achieved with "and" (**ca** = च), occasionally with "or" (**vā** = वा) or "each."
:::

## 5.4. Vocabulary List

**Learn the following words:**

**abhiniveṣa** m. = अभिनिवेष : inclination, tenacity, insistence; specifically: attachment to the body as if it were one’s own.

**kāma** m. = काम : wish, desire, desired gift, sensual pleasure, love; the god of love, Kāma.

(Image source: Wikipedia, Public domain)
:::

**krodha** m. = क्रोध : anger.

**kleśa** m. = क्लेश : torment, affliction.

**trayī** f. = त्रयी : triad; in particular, the three Vedas (Ṛgveda, Sāmaveda, Yajurveda).

**daṇḍanīti** f. = दण्डनीति : politics (a Tatpuruṣa compound from daṇḍa m. = “staff, power, rule, punishment” and nīti f. = “right guidance”).

**dvijāti** 3 / **dvija** 3 = द्विजाति / द्विज : twice-born.

**dveṣa** m. = द्वेष : hatred.

**maitrī** f. = मैत्री : friendship, friendliness, benevolent goodwill.

**rāga** m. = राग : (red) color, passion, love.

**lobha** m. = लोभ : greed, avarice.

**varṇa** m. = वर्ण : color, genus, social class.

**vārttā** f. = वार्त्ता : livelihood, economy (economics).

**vidyā** f. = विद्या : knowledge, science.

**avidyā** f. = अविद्या : ignorance, lack of knowledge.

**ca** = च : and.
(It follows the word it connects. When multiple words are linked, it ideally stands after the first word of the final linking element: brāhmaṇāḥ kṣatriyā vaiśyāḥ śūdrāś ca = ब्राह्मणाः क्षत्रिया वैश्याः शूद्राश्च).

**ca ... ca** = च ... च : both ... and.

**A) Translate the following sentences and compounds, and resolve the Dvandvas occurring therein into Sanskrit:**

1. catvāro varṇā brāhmaṇakṣatriyavaiśyaśūdrāḥ.
   (Āpastambīyadharmasūtra I,1,1,4 = Vāsiṣṭhadharmaśāstra II,1)
   चत्वारो वर्णा ब्राह्मणक्षत्रियवैश्यशूद्राः ||
   (Explanation: catvāras = चत्वारस् = "four")

2. trayo varṇā dvijātayo brāhmaṇakṣatriyavaiśyāḥ.
   (Vāsiṣṭhadharmaśāstra II,1)
   त्रयो वर्णा द्विजातयो ब्राह्मणक्षत्रियवैश्याः ||
   (Explanation: trayas = त्रयस् = "three")

3. sāmavedargvedayajurvedās trayī.
   (Kauṭilīya-arthaśāstra 1.3.1. / in good Sanskrit: sāmārgyajurvedās trayī)
   सामवेदर्ग्वेदयजुर्वेदास्त्रयी ||
   (in good Sanskrit: सामार्यजुर्वेदास्त्रयी)

4. The three enemies of man, which form the gate to hell (Viṣṇusmṛti 33,1+6):
   kāmakrodhalobhāḥ
   कामक्रोधलोभाः ||

5. maitrīkaruṇāmuditopekṣāś catvāro brahmavihārāḥ.
   मैत्रीकरुणामुदितोपेक्षाश्चत्वारो ब्रह्मविहाराः ||
   (Explanation: brahmavihāra: "states of abiding in Brahman," also called the "Immeasurables." These are Buddhist meditation practices.)

6. avidyāsmitārāgadveṣābhiniveṣāḥ pañca kleśāḥ.
   (Yogasūtra 2,3)
   अविद्यास्मितारागद्वेषाभिनिवेषाः पञ्च क्लेशाः ||
   (Explanation: pañca = "five")

7. ānvīkṣikītrayīvārttādaṇḍanitayo vidyāḥ.
   (According to Kauṭilīya-arthaśāstra 1.2.1.)
   आन्वीक्षिकीत्रयीवार्त्तादण्डनितयो विद्याः ||
