Note: ¹ The endings of the 2nd and 3rd singular persons can be replaced by *-tāt* when expressing a blessing or wish. *-tāt* occurs (also in the 3rd singular person) attached to the weak stem.

::: grammar-box
| **Regarding the form of the 2nd singular Imperative ending:** ||
| :---: | :--- |
| **\-ø** | Roots of the 5th and 8th classes, where a final *-u* is preceded only by a consonant. |
| **\-āna** | Roots of the 9th class ending in a consonant, substitute *-āna* for *-nī* + ending. |
| **\-hi** | All other present stems ending in a vowel or semivowel (exception: **jūhudi** from **hu 3**). |
| **\-dhi** | All other cases. |
:::

### 48.2.2. Form of the Present Stem

::: grammar-box
**Strong stem:**

*   **all 1st person forms of the imperative**
*   **3rd sg. imperative**

**Weak stem: all remaining forms**
:::

## 48.3. On Nominal Derivation: तद्धित (Taddhita) Suffixes -a and -ya

::: grammar-box
The suffixes **-a** and (less frequently) **-ya** can be used to derive a noun from another noun. In this process, the first syllable of the original noun assumes the lengthened grade (**Vṛddhi**, **वृद्धि**). If the original stem already ends in **-a**, then **Vṛddhi** is the sole marker of derivation, as the final sound of the stem remains unchanged.

The derived words carry the meaning:

> "having some relation to that designated by the base word"

For example:

*   "descended from"
*   "belonging to"

The words formed in this manner are adjectives but can be substantivized, for example, as patronymics (name formation based on the father: "son of N.N.") or abstract nouns (mostly neuter).
:::

Examples:

::: indent
| Base Word | Derivation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **शुचि** 3 "radiant, pure" | **शौच** n. "purity" |
| **पुत्र** m. "son" | **पौत्र** m. "descended from a son = grandson" |
| **गोतम** m. "owner of many cattle" (proper name) | **गौतम** m. "son of Gotama" |
| **ब्रह्मन्** n. "formulated truth, Veda, Absolute" | **ब्राह्मण** m. "formulator of truth, Brahmin" |
| **शूर** 3 "heroic" | **शौर्य** n. "heroism, bravery" |
| **राजन्** m. "king" | **राज्य** n. "kingdom" |
| **देव** m. "heavenly being, god" | **दैव्य** 3 "heavenly" |
| **ग्राम** m. "village" | **ग्राम्य** 3 "village-related" |
:::

::: grammar-box
| **Treatment of the stem-final sound before the suffix \-a:** ||
| :---: | :--- |
| **\-ṛ** | **\-a** generally attaches to the final **\-r**:[[br]]e.g., **जेतृ** "winner" » **जैत्र** 3 "victorious" |
| **\-a** | Replacement of the **\-a** of the base word by the new suffix **\-a**.[[br]]See examples above. |
| **\-i** | Loss of **\-i**[[br]]e.g., **प्रकृति** f. "nature" » **प्राकृत** 3 "natural, normal, usual, vulgar" |
| **\-u** | mostly: **\-av-a**[[br]]e.g., **गुरु** 3 "heavy"; m. "master, teacher" » **गौरव** 3 "belonging to a master, descended from a master"; n. "weight, heaviness, dignity" |
| **other declension stems:** | see Wackernagel, Altindische Grammatik II,2 § 38 |

The stem-final sound before the suffix **-ya** is treated similarly to that before the suffix **-a**.
:::

See examples above!

::: grammar-box
**Derivations can also be formed from compounds using these suffixes.**

For example, **स्वश्व** m. "the one whose horses are good" (proper name) » **सौवश्व** m. "descendant of Svaśva"

**In compounds where, due to Sandhi, a final -i or -u in the first member is replaced by -y or -v and thus precedes the first vowel of the base word (e.g., compounds with ni-, vi-, su-), the Vṛddhi is formed as if -iy or -uv were present.**

For example, **व्याघ्र** m. "tiger" » **वैयाघ्र** 3 "descended from a tiger, belonging to a tiger"
:::

B) Translate into Sanskrit (using the imperative and preferably roots of the 2nd and 3rd present classes):

1. After you have obtained a son, abandon the family!
2. Descendants of Puru, fear those who have done evil!
3. The girls should give food to the beggars.
4. We want to speak.

8. Tell!
9. Measure out the hells!

11. The tiger-like men should kill those who are hostile to Indra.
12. Concentrate!
13. Sit here!
