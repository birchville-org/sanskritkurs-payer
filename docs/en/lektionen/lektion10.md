## 10.1. The Passive Sentence

::: grammar-box
**Schema:**
**(Agent - kartṛ - in the Instrumental case (tṛtīyā)) - (direct object - karman - in the Nominative case) - passive verbal form**

e.g., **brāhmaṇena deva ijyate** = ब्राह्मणेन देव इज्यते = "A Brahmin worships a god with a sacrifice" (literally: "A god is worshipped by a Brahmin with a sacrifice.")

This sentence is **completely (!) semantically equivalent** to the sentence:
**brāhmaṇo devaṃ yajati / yajate** = ब्राह्मणो देवं यजति / यजते
:::

::: grammar-box
- In a passive sentence, the agent (**kartṛ**) appears in the **Instrumental case** (tṛtīyā f. "third case ending"), and the direct object (**karman** n.) appears in the **Nominative case** (prathamā f.).
- Passive sentences in which the agent is not specified usually carry an impersonal meaning ("one"):
  e.g., **ijyate** = इज्यते "One sacrifices" (literally: "it is worshipped with a sacrifice").
:::

::: grammar-box
**Even if the corresponding active sentence would contain an Accusative (dvitīyā) of the goal, this may be placed in the Nominative (prathamā) in the passive sentence:**

The active sentence **rāmo grāmaṃ gacchati** = रामो ग्रामं गच्छति = "Rāma goes to the village" corresponds to the following passive sentences:
- **rāmeṇa grāmaṃ gamyate** = रामेण ग्रामं गम्यते
- alternatively: **rāmeṇa grāmo gamyate** = रामेण ग्रामो गम्यते
:::

::: grammar-box
**Intransitive verbs (verbs without a direct object) frequently have passive constructions, especially in polite requests:**
- **praviśyatām** = प्रविश्यताम् = "One may enter = Please enter = Come in!"
- **niṣadyatām** = निषद्यताम् = "One may sit = Please sit = Please take a seat"
:::

Sanskrit passive constructions are extremely frequent: the passive is, after all, easier to form than, for example, numerous present stems.

::: important
**Sanskrit passive sentences generally must not be rendered in German using a passive construction, as the German passive serves a completely different stylistic function.**
:::

### 10.2.1. On the Usage of the Instrumental (tṛtīyā = तृतीया)

::: grammar-box
**The Instrumental (tṛtīyā) primarily answers the questions: By what? With what? With whom?**

It is used:
1. In passive constructions: **Agent (kartṛ) in the Instrumental**.
2. To specify the **means or instrument** with which something is accomplished.
3. To specify the **path** along which someone arrives somewhere.
4. To express **connection, union, or accompaniment**.
5. With postpositions meaning "together with, with"; e.g., **saha** = सह = "with" (placed postpositionally).
   - Example: **brāhmaṇena saha** = ब्राह्मणेन सह = "together with the Brahmin"
:::

## 10.3. The so-called Cerebralisationsgesetz for -n- (a word-sandhi)

::: grammar-box
An **-n-** that is followed by a vowel or **n, m, y, v** is replaced by **-ṇ-** if **ṛ, ṝ, r, ṣ** precede the **-n-** within the word, or if no other sound than vowels, gutturals, labials, **y, v, h**, or Anusvāra stands between these sounds and the following **-n-** within the word.
:::

**Therefore:**

::: grammar-box
| | Instrumental Singular[[br]]तृतीया एकवचन |
| :--- | :--- |
| **guru**[[br]]गुरु | **guruṇā**[[br]]गुरुणा |
| **śūdra**[[br]]शूद्र | **śūdreṇa**[[br]]शूद्रेण |
| **īśvara**[[br]]ईश्वर | **īśvereṇa**[[br]]ईश्वरेण |
| **kṣatriya**[[br]]क्षत्रिय | **kṣatriyeṇa**[[br]]क्षत्रियेण |
| **naraka**[[br]]नरक | **narakeṇa**[[br]]नरकेण |
:::

**Thus, the -ṇ- is also to be explained in:**

> **śravaṇa** श्रवण
>
> **śṛṇoti** शृणोति
>
> **kāraṇa** कारण
>
> **brāhmaṇa** ब्राह्मण

## 10.4. The Passive Voice, Indicative Present (yak = यक्)

::: grammar-box
**Formation:**
**(usually) Guṇa-grade root + -ya- + Ātmanepada ending**

**Examples:**

| Root[[br]]धातु | 3rd sg. Pass. Pres. Ind.[[br]]यक् लट् | 3rd pl. Pass. Pres. Ind.[[br]]यक् लट् |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **nī**[[br]]Class 1[[br]](nayati) | **nīyate**[[br]](नीयते)[[br]]"he/she/it is led" | **nīyante**[[br]](नीयन्ते) |
| **man**[[br]]Class 4 Ā[[br]](manyate) | **manyate**[[br]](मन्यते)[[br]]"he/she/it is thought" | **manyante**[[br]](मन्यन्ते) |
| **viś**[[br]]Class 6 P[[br]](viśati) | **viśyate**[[br]](विश्यते)[[br]]"it is entered" | **viśyante**[[br]](विश्यन्ते) |

::: note-box
For verbs of the 4th present class, the Ātmanepada and the passive forms are identical! This can only be distinguished through the sentence construction.
:::

---

### 10.4.1. Special Rules for the Formation of the Passive

::: grammar-box
1. **Roots ending in -i or -u** lengthen their vowel before the passive suffix **-ya-**:
   * **ji** 1 P: **jīyate**, **jīyante**[[br]]जीयते, जीयन्ते
   * **śru** 5 P: **śrūyate**, **śrūyante**[[br]]श्रूयते, श्रूयन्ते
   * **su** 5 U: **sūyate**, **sūyante**[[br]]सूयते, सूयन्ते

2. **Roots with -a- between consonants** (except nasals, y, r, v) remain high-grade:
   * **labh** 1 Ā: **labhyate**, **labhyante**[[br]]लभ्यते, लभ्यन्ते

3. **Some roots form their passive in the high-grade** (or in the ablaut stage of the present stem):
   * **āp** 5 P: **āpyate**, **āpyante**[[br]]आप्यते, आप्यन्ते
   * **khād** 1 P: **khādyate**, **khādyante**[[br]]खाद्यते, खाद्यन्ते
   * **smṛ** 1 P: **smaryate**, **smaryante**[[br]]स्मर्यते, स्मर्यन्ते

4. **Low-grade of verbs beginning with ya, va, ra, or where these sounds follow another consonant (Samprasāraṇa = सम्प्रसारण):**

   | Root[[br]]धातु | Low-grade[[br]]सम्प्रसारण | Passive[[br]]यक् |
   | :--- | :--- | :--- |
   | **yaj** 1 U[[br]]यज् | \*yj &raquo; ij | **ijyate**[[br]]इज्यते[[br]]**ijyante**[[br]]इज्यन्ते |
   | **vad** 1 P[[br]]वद् | \*vd &raquo; ud | **udyate**[[br]]उद्यते[[br]]**udyante**[[br]]उद्यन्ते |
   | **prach** 6 P[[br]]प्रच्छ् | \*prcch &raquo; pṛcch | **pṛcchyate**[[br]]पृच्छ्यते[[br]]**pṛcchyante**[[br]]पृच्छ्यन्ते |

   The traditional indigenous term for this formation of the low-grade in verbs with **ya** or **va** is **Samprasāraṇa** (नपुंसकम् = सम्प्रसारण).

5. **Final -ṛ** after a single consonant is replaced by **-ri-** before the passive suffix **-ya-**:
   * **kṛ** 8 U: **kriyate**, **kriyante**[[br]]क्रियते, क्रियन्ते
   * However: **smṛ** 1 P: **smaryate**[[br]]स्मर्यते

6. **Some roots ending in -an** alternatively have two passive stem formations:
   * one **-an-ya**
   * one **-ā-ya-** (the long **ā** represents a hypothetical long nasal)

   e.g., **tan** 8 U.
   * **tan-ya-te**, **tan-ya-nte**[[br]]तन्यते, तन्यन्ते
   * **tā-ya-te**, **tā-ya-nte**[[br]]तायते, तायन्ते (from \*tn-ya-nte)
:::
