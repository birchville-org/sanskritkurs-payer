# Lesson 20

## 20.1. Possessive Compounds = बहुव्रीहि m.

::: media
![](/images/lekt2004.jpg)
Fig.: बहुव्रीहिः पुरुषः
(Image source: [Details](/licenses#lekt2004))
:::

बहुव्रीहिः = बहवो व्रीहयो यस्य सः = "one who possesses much rice"

:::: grammar-box

**In contrast to a Tatpuruṣa, a Bahuvrīhi denotes something other than what is expressed by its members: while बालपुत्रः as a Tatpuruṣa resolves to "a young son," i.e., something expressed by the final member of the compound (पुत्र), बालपुत्रः as a Bahuvrīhi resolves to "one whose son is young," i.e., the denoted entity is neither the son (पुत्र) nor necessarily young (बाल), but a person distinct from the young son, namely his father.**

**Possessive compounds are initially always adjectives, which can, however, be substantivized. Therefore, the grammatical gender of a Bahuvrīhi depends on what the Bahuvrīhi refers to, and not on the gender of the final member of the compound.**

::::

:::: grammar-box

**Schema for resolving a Bahuvrīhi:**

**1st member usually in the Nominative (प्रथमा) Singular, Dual, or Plural -- 2nd member in the Nominative Singular, Dual, or Plural -- Relative pronoun in a case other than the Nominative (usually Genitive - षष्ठी) and in the gender and number of the total Bahuvrīhi -- Demonstrative pronoun in the number, case, and gender of the total Bahuvrīhi.**

::::

Examples:


::: indent
गतपापः = गतं पापं यस्य सः = "one whose evil has gone" = "one who is free from misdeeds"

Acc. sg. गतपापम् = गतं पापं यस्य तम्

Inst. sg. गतपापेन = गतं पापं यस्य तेन

Gen. sg. गतपापस्य = गतं पापं यस्य तस्य

Nom. pl. गतपापाः = गतानि पापानि yeषां ते

etc.

अस्तमोहा = अस्तो मोहो यया सा = "a (woman) who has cast off delusion"

प्राप्तोदको ग्रामः = प्राप्तमुदकं यं s ग्रामः = "A village to which water has come" = "a village threatened by flooding"

पुण्यवत्पुत्रः as a Bahuvrīhi = पुण्यवान्पुत्रो यस्य सः, or: पुण्यवन्तः पुत्रा यस्य सः = "one whose son possesses merit" or: "one whose sons possess merit"
:::

:::: grammar-box

**The relationship between the first and second members of a Bahuvrīhi can be:**

*   **attributive**
*   **appositional**
*   **casual**
*   **adverbial**

**As with a Tatpuruṣa, the more specifically determined member usually stands in the second position.**

::::

Traditionally, one distinguishes:

*   समानाधिकरणबहुव्रीहिः : in the विग्रहवाक्य (resolution sentence), the first and second members stand in the same case
    
*   व्यधिकरणबहुव्रीहिः : in the विग्रहवाक्य (resolution sentence), the first and second members stand in different cases

## 20.2. Bahuvrīhi with an Attributive First Member

:::: grammar-box

**Schema:**

::: indent
**Adjective -- Noun**
:::

**Example:**

::: indent
गुणवत्पुत्रो ब्राह्मणः = गुणवान्पुत्रो यस्य स ब्राह्मणः = "a Brāhmaṇa whose son(s) possess(es)/possess good qualities"
:::

**Attributive determination by a Past Participle (PPP), which is rare in Tatpuruṣa compounds, occurs particularly frequently. In the resolution (pada-pāṭha), the relative pronoun usually appears in the Instrumental case (तृतीया) (more rarely in the Genitive / षष्ठी).**

**Rule of Thumb:**

*   **PPP - Noun = mostly Bahuvrīhi**
*   **Noun -- PPP = mostly Tatpuruṣa**

**Example:**

::: indent
कृतफल ३ = कृतं फलं यena सः । यया सा । यena तत् = "one (m./f./n.) who/which has produced a result, someone/something effective"
:::

::::

## 20.3. Bahuvrīhi with Appositional First Member

:::: grammar-box

**Schema:**

::: indent
**Noun -- Noun**
:::

**Examples:**

::: indent
शूरपुत्रो नरः = शूरा एव पुत्रा यस्य स नरः = "a man whose sons are heroes"

तदन्त ३ = सो ऽन्तो यस्य सः । यस्याः सा । यस्य तत् = "the one whose end is this" = "ending with that"
:::

::::

:::: grammar-box

**Appositional Bahuvrīhi compounds with the second members आदि, आदिक, and आद्य meaning "first, beginning" are of great importance, and less frequently प्रभृति meaning "beginning." Such compounds correspond to "etc.":**

**Example:**

::: indent
देवा इन्द्रादयः =  इन्द्र आदिर्येषां ते देवाः = "the gods, whose beginning is Indra" = "the gods Indra and others" = "Indra and the remaining gods"
:::

**To express "only," one can use bahuvrīhi compounds with मात्रा (f.) meaning "measure, limit" as the second member:**

**Example:**

::: indent
शब्दमात्रम् = शब्दो मात्रा यस्य तत् = "that whose measure is a word" = "only a word"
:::

**मात्र following a past passive participle (PPP) is to be translated as "as soon as":**

**Example:**

::: indent
जातमात्रं शत्रुं घ्नन्ति = जातं मात्रा यस्य तं शत्रुं घ्नन्ति = "they strike down an enemy whose measure is having come into being" = "they strike down the enemy as soon as he has come into being"
:::

## 20.4. Bahuvrīhi with a Case-Formed First Member

i.e., Bahuvrīhi compounds in which the first member stands in a case other than the nominative (प्रथमा).

Example:

::: indent
देवरूपा = देवस्यैव रूपं यस्याः सा = "a (woman) whose form is that of a god" "a woman of divine appearance"
:::

:::: grammar-box

**Exception to the general rule regarding the order of compounding members:**

**The case-marked member occupies the second position when it denotes a body part (especially the hand):**

**Example:**

::: indent
दण्डहस्तः =  दण्डो हस्ते (Lok. sg.) यस्य सः = "one in whose hand there is a staff" = "one who carries a staff in his hand."
:::

## 20.5. Bahuvrīhi with an Adverbial First Member

The first member consists of an adverb, a preposition, a preverb, or another indeclinable; such compounds are generally **nityasamāsa** (etymologically fixed compounds).

Examples:

::: indent
aputro naraḥ = putro yasya nāsti sa naraḥ = "a man who has no son" = "a sonless man"

durbala ³ = "someone/something whose strength is poor" = "weak, feeble"
:::

:::: grammar-box

**The Bahuvrīhi with the first member सह ("with") is often replaced by स.**

**Example:**

::: indent
saputraḥ = sahaputraḥ = putreṇa sahitaḥ । putreṇa sah = "with a son," "accompanied by a son"
:::

## 20.6. Inflection of the Final Member of a Bahuvrīhi

:::: grammar-box

**The final member of a Bahuvrīhi is inflected as an adjective of three genders, independent of its original grammatical gender. Thus, if the final member of a compound has a gender different from its original one, it must be a Bahuvrīhi, unless it is a Samāhāradvanva (neuter singular).**

**In the process of adjectivalization:**

*   **-ā-stems become -a-stems in the masculine and neuter**
*   **-a-stems (m., n.) form their feminine on -ā or -ī**
*   **-ī-stems (f.) must take the suffix -ka, -kā, -ka (n.) at the end of Bahuvrīhis; this suffix can or must also be taken by many other Bahuvrīhis**

::::

## 20.7. Another Classification of Types of Bahuvrīhis

The examples here are provided for later study; for some of them, the necessary grammar and vocabulary have not yet been discussed.

1.  Qualifying word in the first member — qualified word in the second member: स्थिरचित्तः = स्थिरं चित्तं यस्य सः
2.  Indeclinables in the first member: उच्चैःश्रवस्
3.  Comparison in the first member: कुशाग्रधीः = कुशाग्र इव धीर्यस्य सः
4.  Implicit comparison: उष्ट्रमुखः = उष्ट्रस्य मुखमिव मुखं यस्य सः = "camel-faced" = whose face is like that of a camel
5.  Numerals as the second member for approximate numbers: उपसशाः = दशानां समीपे ये सन्ति ते = "approximately ten"
6.  Both members are numerals: एकद्वाः = एको वा द्वौ वा = "one or two"
7.  With sa-/saha- in the first member: सपुत्रः । सहपुत्रः
8.  With sa- (= समान) in the first member: सजनपदः = समानो जनपदो यस्य सः
9.  नञ्बहुव्रीहिः = with a-/an- in the first member: अपुत्रः = न विद्यते पुत्रो यस्य सः ; अनङ्गः = न विद्यते अङ्गं यस्य सः
10. With prepositions and similar elements in the first member: प्रवातः = प्रकृष्टो वातो यस्मिन् सः ; निर्धनः = निर्गतं धनं यस्मात्सः ; सुबुद्धिः = सुष्ठु बुद्धिर्यस्य सः ; दुर्बुद्धिः = दुष्टा बुद्धिर्यस्य सः
11. Directional terms used to denote intermediate directions: पुर्वोत्तरा "northeast"
12. Past Participles (PPP) as the first member: कृतकटः = कृतः कटो येन सः
13. And others

## 20.8. Relationship between Bahuvrīhi and Relative Clause

Although every Bahuvrīhi can be resolved into a relative clause, not every relative clause can be replaced by a Bahuvrīhi. Hermann Georg Jacobi (1850–1937), who examined the relationship between the two in greater detail (*Compositum und Nebensatz*, 1897), writes:

::: indent
"When translating from German or another modern language into Sanskrit, not all subordinate clauses can be rendered as compounds, but only those that are embellishing and descriptive; however, those that contain a conceptually necessary or important determination appear in Sanskrit as relative clauses."
:::

::: media
![](/images/lekt2002.jpg)
Fig.: Hermann Jacobi
(Image source: [Details](/licenses#lekt2002))
:::

This may be true. However, the Indians then regard as embellishing and descriptive what we would consider "conceptually necessary or important."

लोकानां करदा मही ॥

**Explanation:** *-da* at the end of a compound: "giving"

On matters of property:

::: indent
भार्या पुत्रश्च दासश्च
त्रय एवाधनाः स्मृताः ।
यत्ते समधिगच्छन्ति
यस्य ते तस्य तद्धनम् ॥मनुस्मृति ८.४१६॥

**Explanation:** त्रयस् = Nom. masc. pl. of त्रि "three"
:::

A *su bhāṣitam* on female beauty:

::: indent
तनुमध्यं पृथुश्रोणि
रक्तौष्ठमसितेक्षणम् ।
नतनाभि वपुः स्त्रीणां
कं न हन्त्युन्नतस्तनम् ॥

**Explanation:** All forms except कं and स्त्रीणाम् are Nom. sg. neut. and refer to वपुस्.
