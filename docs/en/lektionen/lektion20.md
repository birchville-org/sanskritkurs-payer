# Lesson 20

## 20.1. Possessive Compounds = ⟪बहुव्रीहि⟫ m.

::: media
![](/images/lekt2004.webp)
Fig.: ⟪बहुव्रीहिः पुरुषः⟫
Jodhpur = ⟪जोधपुर⟫
(Image source: [Details](/en/licenses#lekt2004))
:::
⟪बहुव्रीहिः⟫ = ⟪बहवो व्रीहयो यस्य सः⟫ = "one who possesses much rice"

::: grammar-box

**In contrast to a Tatpuruṣa, a Bahuvrīhi denotes something other than what is expressed by its members: while ⟪बालपुत्रः⟫ as a Tatpuruṣa resolves to "a young son," i.e., something expressed by the final member of the compound (⟪पुत्र⟫), ⟪बालपुत्रः⟫ as a Bahuvrīhi resolves to "one whose son is young," i.e., the denoted entity is neither the son (⟪पुत्र⟫) nor necessarily young (⟪बाल⟩), but a person distinct from the young son, namely his father.**

**Possessive compounds are initially always adjectives, which can however be substantivized. Therefore, the grammatical gender of a Bahuvrīhi depends on what the Bahuvrīhi refers to, and not on the gender of the final compound member.**

:::
::: grammar-box

**Schema for resolving a Bahuvrīhi:**

**1. First member usually in the Nominative (⟪प्रथमा⟫) Singular, Dual or Plural -- 2. Member in the Nominative Singular, Dual or Plural -- Relative pronoun in a case other than the Nominative (usually Genitive - ⟪षष्ठी⟫) and in gender and number of the total Bahuvrīhi -- Demonstrative pronoun in number, case and gender of the total Bahuvrīhi.**

:::
Examples:


::: indent
⟪गतपापः⟫ = ⟪गतं पापं यस्य सः⟫ = "one whose evil has gone" = "one who is free from misdeeds"

Acc. sg. ⟪गतपापम्⟫ = ⟪गतं पापं यस्य तम्⟫

Inst. sg. ⟪गतपापेन⟫ = ⟪गतं पापं यस्य तेन⟫

Gen. sg. ⟪गतपापस्य⟫ = ⟪गतं पापं यस्य तस्य⟫

Nom. pl. ⟪गतपापाः⟫ = ⟪गतानि पापानि⟫ ye⟪षां ते⟫

etc.

⟪अस्तमोहा⟫ = ⟪अस्तो मोहो यया सा⟫ = "a (woman) who has cast off delusion"

⟪प्राप्तोदको ग्रामः⟫ = ⟪प्राप्तमुदकं यं⟫ s ⟪ग्रामः⟫ = "a village to which the water has reached" = "a village threatened by flooding"

⟪पुण्यवत्पुत्रः⟫ as Bahuvrīhi = ⟪पुण्यवान्पुत्रो यस्य सः⟫, or: ⟪पुण्यवन्तः पुत्रा यस्य सः⟫ = "one whose son possesses merit" or: "one whose sons possess merit"
:::
::: grammar-box

**The relationship between the first member and the second member in a Bahuvrīhi can be:**

*   **attributive**
*   **appositional**
*   **casual**
*   **adverbial**

**As with the Tatpuruṣa, the specified member usually stands in second place.**

:::
Traditionally, one distinguishes:

*   ⟪समानाधिकरणबहुव्रीहिः⟫ : in ⟪विग्रहवाक्य⟫ the first member and the second member are in the same case

*   ⟪व्यधिकरणबहुव्रीहिः⟫ : in ⟪विग्रहवाक्य⟫ the first member and the second member are in different cases


## 20.2. Bahuvrīhi with attributive first member

::: grammar-box

**Schema:**

:::
::: indent
**Adjective -- Noun**
:::
**Example:**

::: indent
⟪गुणवत्पुत्रो ब्राह्मणः⟫ =  ⟪गुणवान्पुत्रो यस्य स ब्राह्मणः⟫ = "a Brāhmaṇa whose son / sons possesses / possess good qualities"
:::
**Attributive determination by a PPP (Past Participle), which is rare in Tatpuruṣa, occurs particularly frequently. The relative pronoun then usually stands in the Instrumental (⟪तृतीया⟫) upon resolution (rarely in the Genitive / ⟪षष्ठी⟫).**

**Rule of thumb:**

*   **PPP - noun = mostly Bahuvrīhi**
*   **noun -- PPP = mostly Tatpuruṣa**

**Example:**

::: indent
⟪कृतफल ३⟫ = ⟪कृतं फलं⟫ ye⟪न सः । यया सा ।⟫ ye⟪न तत्⟫ = "one / a / an, who / which has produced a result, someone / something effective"
:::
## 20.3. Bahuvrīhi with appositional first member

::: grammar-box

**Schema:**

:::
::: indent
**noun -- noun**
:::
**Examples:**

::: indent
⟪शूरपुत्रो नरः⟫ = ⟪शूरा एव पुत्रा यस्य स नरः⟫ = "a man whose sons are heroes"

⟪तदन्त ३⟫ = ⟪सो ऽन्तो यस्य सः । यस्याः सा । यस्य तत्⟫ = "who / which, whose end is this" = "ending with that"
:::
::: grammar-box

**Very important are appositional Bahuvrīhis with the second members ⟪आदि । आदिक । आद्य⟫ "first, beginning", less frequently ⟪प्रभृति⟫ "beginning". Such compounds correspond to "etc.":**

**Example:**

:::
::: indent
⟪देवा इन्द्रादयः⟫ =  ⟪इन्द्र आदिर्येषां ते देवाः⟫ = "the gods whose beginning is Indra" = "Indra etc." = "Indra and the other gods"
:::
**To express "only", one can use bahuvrīhis with ⟪मात्रा⟫ f. "measure, limit" as the second member:**

**Example:**

::: indent
⟪शब्दमात्रम्⟫ = ⟪शब्दो मात्रा यस्य तत्⟫ = "that whose measure is a word" = "only one word"
:::
**⟪मात्र⟫ after a PPP is to be translated as "as soon as":**

**Example:**

::: indent
⟪जातमात्रं शत्रुं घ्नन्ति⟫ = ⟪जातं मात्रा यस्य तं शत्रुं घ्नन्ति⟫ = "they slay an enemy whose measure is arising" = "they slay the enemy as soon as it has arisen"
:::

## 20.4. Bahuvrīhi with a case-marked first member

i.e., Bahuvrīhis whose first member represents a case other than the nominative (⟪प्रथमा⟫).

Example:

::: indent
⟪देवरूपा⟫ = ⟪देवस्यैव रूपं यस्याः सा⟫ = "a (woman) whose form is that of a god" "a woman with divine appearance"
:::
::: grammar-box

**Exception to the general rule regarding the order of compound members:**

**The case-marked member stands in second place when it denotes a body part (esp. the hand):**

**Example:**

:::
::: indent
⟪दण्डहस्तः⟫ =  ⟪दण्डो हस्ते⟫ (Loc. sg.) ⟪यस्य सः⟫ = "one in whose hand a staff is located" = "one who carries a staff in the hand."
:::

## 20.5. Bahuvrīhi with an adverbial first member

An adverb, a preposition, a prefix, or another indeclinable stands in the first member; such compounds are generally ⟪नित्यसमास⟫.

Examples:

::: indent
⟪अपुत्रो नरः⟫ = ⟪पुत्रो यस्य नास्ति स नरः⟫ = "a man who has no son" = "a sonless man"

⟪दुर्बल ३⟫ = "someone / something whose strength is bad" = "powerless, weak"
:::
::: grammar-box

**⟪सह⟫ "with" as the first member of a Bahuvrīhi is often replaced by ⟪स⟫.**

**Example:**

:::
::: indent
⟪सपुत्रः⟫ =  ⟪सहपुत्रः⟫ =  ⟪पुत्रेण सहितः । पुत्रेण सह्⟫ = "with a son", "accompanied by a son"
:::

## 20.6. Inflection of the final member of a Bahuvrīhi

::: grammar-box

**The final member of a Bahuvrīhi is inflected as an adjective with multiple genders, regardless of its original gender. Thus, if in a compound the final member has a different gender than its original one, it must be a Bahuvrīhi if it is not a Samāhāradvanva (Neuter singular).**

**When adjectivizing,**

*   **-ā-stems become -a-stems in the Masculine and Neuter**
*   **-a-stems (m., n.) form their Feminine on -ā or -ī**
*   **-ī-stems (f.) must take the suffix -ka, -kā, -ka (n.) at the end of Bahuvrīhis; this suffix can or must also be taken by many other Bahuvrīhis**

:::
## 20.7. Another classification of the types of Bahuvrīhis

The examples are given here for later study; for some, the necessary grammar and vocabulary have not yet been discussed

1. qualifying word in the first member -- qualified word in the second member: ⟪स्थिरचित्तः⟫ = ⟪स्थिरं चित्तं यस्य सः⟫
2. indeclinables in the first member: ⟪उच्चैःश्रवस्⟫
3. comparison in the first member: ⟪कुशाग्रधीः⟫ =  ⟪कुशाग्र इव धीर्यस्य सः⟫
4. implicit comparison: ⟪उष्ट्रमुखः⟫ = ⟪उष्ट्रस्य मुखमिव मुखं यस्य सः⟫ = "camel-mouthed" = whose mouth is like that of a camel
5. numeral as the second member for approximate number: ⟪उपसशाः⟫ = ⟪दशानां समीपे ये सन्ति ते⟫ = "about ten"
6. both members are numerals: ⟪एकद्वाः⟫ = ⟪एको वा द्वौ वा⟫ = "one or two"
7. with sa-/saha- in the first member: ⟪सपुत्रः । सहपुत्रः⟫
8. with sa- (= ⟪समान⟫) in the first member: ⟪सजनपदः⟫ = ⟪समानो जनपदो यस्य सः⟫
9. ⟪नञ्बहुव्रीहिः⟫ = with a-/an- in the first member: ⟪अपुत्रः⟫ = ⟪न विद्यते पुत्रो यस्य सः⟫ ; ⟪अनङ्गः⟫ = ⟪न विद्यते अङ्गं यस्य सः⟫
10. with preposition and similar in the first member: ⟪प्रवातः⟫ =⟪प्रकृष्टो वातो यस्मिन् सः⟫ ; ⟪निर्धनः⟫ = ⟪निर्गतं धनं यस्मात्सः⟫ ; ⟪सुबुद्धिः⟫ = ⟪सुष्ठु बुद्धिर्यस्य सः⟫ ; ⟪दुर्बुद्धिः⟫ = ⟪दुष्टा बुद्धिर्यस्य सः⟫
11. directional terms to indicate intermediate directions: ⟪पुर्वोत्तरा⟫ "northeast"
12. PPP as the first member: ⟪कृतकटः⟫ = ⟪कृतः कटो येन सः⟫
13. and others

## 20.8. Relationship between Bahuvrīhi and Relative Clause

Although every bahuvrīhi can be resolved into a relative clause, not every relative clause can be replaced by a bahuvrīhi. Hermann Georg Jacobi (1850 - 1937), who examined the relationship between the two in greater detail (Compositum und Nebensatz, 1897), writes:

::: indent
"When translating from German or another modern language into Sanskrit, not all subordinate clauses may be rendered by compounds; only the embellishing and descriptive ones are suitable. Those, however, which contain a conceptually necessary or important determination also appear in Sanskrit as relative clauses."
:::
::: media
![](/images/lekt2002.jpg)
Fig.: Hermann Jacobi
(Image source: [Details](/en/licenses#lekt2002))
:::
That may be true. However, the Indians then view many things as embellishing and descriptive that we would consider "conceptually necessary or important."
