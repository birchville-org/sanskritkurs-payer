# Lesson 51

## 51.1. Upapada Compounds

:::: grammar-box
**Upapada compounds (Sanskrit: उपपद, *upapada* n. "accompanying word") are tatpurusha compounds in which the second member is a verbal noun that occurs exclusively as the second member of compounds and never as an independent, standalone word. Upapadas are formed with the kṛt suffixes**

* **-Ø**
* **-a**
* **-t**

**They are nomina agentis, i.e., they denote an agent (कर्तृ, *kartṛ*) who performs the action designated by the underlying root.**

**Such compounds are not resolved by native commentators through nominal combinations, but rather through verbal forms:**

Examples:

::: indent
सुखकरः = सुखं करोतीति सुखकरः

कुलघ्नः = कुलम्हन्तीति कुलघ्नः
:::
::::

Examples:

a) kṛt suffix \-Ø

::: indent
\-nī 3 "leading," e.g., सेनानी m. "army leader" (सेना f. "army")

\-bhuj 3 "enjoying, eating," e.g., भूमिभुज् m. "king" (भूमि f. "earth")

\-vid 3 "knowing," e.g., धर्मविद् 3 "one who knows the Dharma"

## 51.2. Declension of Stems Ending in a Simple Consonant (Excluding Nasals, Semivowels, and -s)

::: grammar-box
**Stems ending in a simple consonant (excluding nasals, semivowels, and -s) exhibit no stem gradation. Their declension proceeds entirely regularly by the addition of standard case endings.**

**The sole irregularity: a nasal is inserted before the stem final in the nominative, accusative, and vocative plural of neuter nouns.**
:::

### 51.2.1. Stems on non-palatal stops

::: grammar-box
**The usual sound change laws apply, i.e.:**
* **In final position, the corresponding voiceless non-aspirate appears.**
* **Before the *-su* of the locative plural, the final sound is initially replaced by the corresponding voiceless non-aspirate, after which the corresponding sound changes occur.**
* **Before a voiced consonant, the final sound is replaced by the corresponding voiced non-aspirate.**
:::

### 51.2.2. Stems ending in palatal stops (c, ch, j), ś, ṣ

::: grammar-box
**If the ending begins with a vowel, the final sound of the stem remains unchanged.**

**For all other endings, the following rules apply:**

* **Final -c and -j are replaced by k, and the stem is then treated as if it ended in -k.**
* **In certain words (see the list in Kielhorn, Grammar, p. 18), final -j is replaced by -ṭ.**
* **Final -ch is replaced by -ṭ.**
* **Final -ś and -ṣ are, with a few exceptions, replaced by -ṭ. In the exceptional cases, -ś and -ṣ are replaced by -k.**

**In other words:**

* **-c » -k**
* **-ch » -ṭ**
* **-j » -k or -ṭ**
* **-ś » -ṭ or -k**
* **-ṣ » -ṭ or -k**
:::

### 51.2.3. Stems ending in -h

::: grammar-box
**Before endings beginning with a vowel, the final -h remains unchanged.**

**Before all other endings:**
* **the -h is normally replaced by -ḍh**
* **if the underlying root begins with d-, the -h is replaced by -gh. The same applies optionally or obligatorily to a few other words (see the compilation in Kielhorn, Grammar §80,2)**
* **(in the absolute final position of the root noun, -h is replaced by -dh)**

**After these replacements, the stem is treated further as if it ended in -ḍh, -gh, or -dh, respectively.**

**See the paradigms in Kielhorn, Grammar, pp. 20f.**

## 51.3. Sandhi of Initial h-

:::: grammar-box
**Before an initial h-, a preceding stop consonant is replaced by the corresponding voiced non-aspirated consonant, and the initial h- is replaced by the corresponding voiced aspirated consonant:**

::: indent
तत् + हि » तद्धि

वाक् + हि » वाग्घि

परिव्राट् + हि » परिव्राड्ढि
:::
::::

## 51.4. Sandhi of Initial ch-

:::: grammar-box
**Initial ch- is replaced by cch- after a short vowel, after मा "not," and after आ "to":**

::: indent
न + छिन्दति » न च्छिन्दति
:::
::::

## 51.5. -ch- in the Middle of Words

:::: grammar-box
**In the middle of words, -ch- is replaced by -cch- after all vowels:**

::: indent
e.g. छिद् » चिच्छेद

ऋतु m.: periodic event, season, time period, menstruation, time during which the woman is fertile and has a right to intercourse with her husband.

::: indent
Regarding ऋतु, see Manu III, 45-48: according to this text, ऋतु lasts 16 days (according to an alternative translation: 20 days) from the onset of menstruation; sexual intercourse is prohibited during the first four days following the onset of bleeding (according to the alternative translation: during the first eight (4 + 4) days), as well as on the 11th (or 15th) and 13th (or 18th) days. On even days, the woman conceives sons; on odd days, daughters. For the following discussion, a ऋतु of 16 days in total is assumed (not the alternative translation), as do most indigenous commentaries, and thus this was the prevailing view.

Since ovulation occurs 14 days before the onset of menstruation, this determination of the fertile period nearly "guarantees" fertility for a menstrual cycle interval of 19 to 30 days. The prohibited days (11th and 13th) improve the probability of intercourse on the 12th and 14th days, i.e., the probability of conception in a 28-day cycle (the lifespan of sperm in the female is approximately 3 days). These regulations can be regarded as a positive application of the Knaus-Ogino method.
